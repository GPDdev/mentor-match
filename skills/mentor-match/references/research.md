# Research and verification

Use this process for new searches and audits. Work sequentially as one agent.

## 1. Build a query map

Create query groups for:

- exact research topics and neighboring terms;
- methods, instruments, datasets, organisms, systems, or application domains;
- opportunity type and cycle terms;
- official institution, school, department, center, laboratory, and program names;
- local-language equivalents and former names when relevant.

Never include unnecessary personal details from the applicant in queries.

## 2. Establish the candidate universe

For institution-scoped searches, start from official faculty, laboratory, program, center, and vacancy directories. Check adjacent or jointly appointed units only when the intake permits them.

For broader searches, combine topical queries with official-domain queries. Public scholarly indexes can identify likely researchers, but must not be the only source for current affiliation or opportunity status.

Search universities, nonprofit/public research institutes, and industry research labs. Classify each organization and identify whether the target opportunity is a degree, employment role, fellowship, visiting role, or joint arrangement.

## 3. Source hierarchy

Use the strongest available source for each claim:

1. Official current program, institution, department, position, funder, or application page.
2. Official laboratory or supervisor page.
3. DOI landing pages, publisher or conference records, Crossref metadata, ORCID public profiles, OpenAlex public records, and comparable scholarly sources.
4. Reputable funder databases, professional societies, institutional news, and verified professional profiles.
5. Forums, social media, anonymous reviews, and aggregators as anecdotal leads only.

Crossref may be used without authentication for public bibliographic metadata. OpenAlex and ORCID APIs may require credentials or impose changing limits; do not make either a required dependency. Use their public web records or other sources when credentials are unavailable. Never ask the user to paste secrets into chat.

## 4. Minimum verification

For every shortlisted person or group, verify:

- identity and current affiliation;
- current role and organization type;
- current research themes, supported by recent work or an actively maintained page;
- compatibility with the requested opportunity type;
- admissions, hiring, funding, and deadline claims when those claims are made.

Use recent evidence where possible. For research direction, emphasize the last three to five years while retaining older foundational work when still central. Record page dates when available and always record the access date.

## 5. Admissions and hiring status

Use these normalized states:

- `open-confirmed`: an official current or future-cycle page explicitly invites applications or lists an open role;
- `open-continuous`: an official page describes ongoing recruitment without a specific slot or cycle;
- `program-route`: admission is through a program or committee and direct supervisor commitment is not established;
- `unknown`: no reliable current evidence;
- `closed`: an official source says applications are closed or the person is not accepting applicants;
- `stale-or-conflicting`: only old evidence exists or current sources disagree.

Do not convert publication activity, grant awards, or team size into an assertion that someone is recruiting.

## 6. Program, funding, and scholarship research

Identify the governing application route before recommending outreach. Capture official deadlines, degree requirements, supervisor-contact rules, funding attached to the program or role, and relevant external scholarships or national schemes. Separate guaranteed, competitive, and merely possible funding.

## 7. Culture and reputation research

Search public discussion when requested, including forums, social media, alumni pages, lab news, and anonymous reviews. For each signal record:

- source type and URL;
- publication date if available;
- whether the writer's relationship to the lab is known;
- whether independent sources corroborate it;
- plausible alternative explanations.

Summarize patterns rather than sensational claims. Do not name private students or repeat unnecessary identifying details. Label single-source and anonymous material low confidence.

## 8. De-duplicate and stop

De-duplicate joint appointments, name variants, renamed laboratories, and multiple pages for the same person. Stop when the requested scope is covered or new searches repeatedly return duplicates, ineligible candidates, or candidates lacking minimum verification. Document uncovered areas, blocked pages, and remaining uncertainty.
